## 1. Abstract & Scope

**Techcapades** maps human positions on technology and digital life — what you think of AI, how you guard your privacy, how much screen you live behind, how you connect online, who you are when you are digital, and what you think the machines are doing to us. In traditional surveys, tech attitudes are collapsed into crude single-axis binaries (e.g. Pro-Tech vs. Anti-Tech; Online vs. Offline) or reduced to a list of apps you use.

Under the **Taxopolemics** engine, tech positions are mapped to their natural geometric degrees of freedom: from **1-Pole scalars** (absolute affinity), to **2-Pole dialectical dipoles** (Privacy vs. Convenience; Accelerate vs. Decelerate), to higher-order **Triads (3-Pole $\Delta^2$)**, **Tetrads (4-Pole $\Delta^3$)**, **Pentads (5-Pole $\Delta^4$)**, **Hexads (6-Pole $\Delta^5$)**, **Heptads (7-Pole $\Delta^6$)**, and **Octads (8-Pole $\Delta^7$)**.

**Engine cap:** the engine goes to **8 poles and caps there** — monopole through octad. The cap is a human limit, not a technical one: people cannot hold more than about eight positions in mind at once (Jason, 2026-11-14).

**Scope note:** Techcapades is the seventh skin. It covers technology and digital life — where people have real positions, those positions are live and contested, and comparing profiles is about compatibility (can we share a screen, a home, a life, when we disagree about the machines?). The ground shifts fast here — a taxonomy of AI attitudes from 2026 may look dated quickly — so the topics are framed as stances, not as product preferences. Techcapades brushes Philoscapades (transhumanism, AI consciousness) and Poliscapades (tech regulation, AI policy, privacy law); the difference is angle. Techcapades asks how *you* live with the machines. Philoscapades asks what the machines *are*. Poliscapades asks what the law *should do* about them.

## 2. Taxonomic Architecture & Rating Widget Assignments

### Category 1: AI & Intelligence

#### 1.1 AI Risk & Benefit Allocation (5-Pole Pentad $\Delta^4$)
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Green #22c55e)`: **Post-Scarcity Flourishing** — Radical abundance, medicine, automation dividends.
  - `Pole 1 (Yellow #eab308)`: **Corporate / Algorithmic Feudalism** — Concentration of capital, surveillance, platform monopoly.
  - `Pole 2 (Orange #f97316)`: **Labor & Cognitive Atrophy** — Erosion of human skills, mass displacement, cultural stagnation.
  - `Pole 3 (Red #ef4444)`: **Existential Misalignment** — Loss of agency, superintelligent runaway, extinction.

## 4. Higher-Pole Candidates (not yet specified)

- **Social Platforms** (pentad or hexad): X / Instagram / TikTok / Reddit / YouTube (+ Facebook). Platforms are a preference list, not a pole structure, and they change fast — may be better as multiple 1-pole topics, or as a platform map. Needs a ruling on whether platforms are positions or just habits.
- **Tech Ethics** (tetrad): Utilitarian / Deontological / Virtue / Care. Overlaps Philoscapades (normative ethics) — needs a ruling on whether it belongs here as applied ethics, or stays in philosophy.
- **AI Risk Positions** (pentad): Existential risk / Misalignment / Misuse / Concentration of power / Benign. A refinement of 1.1 for people who want more granularity on *how* AI goes wrong. Needs a ruling on whether it is distinct from 1.1, or whether 1.1 should expand.

## 5. Open Questions for the Principal Investigator

1. Is the scope right — technology and digital life as one skin, or should AI split off from screens/privacy?
2. Does 1.1 (AI Futures) stay a pentad, or should it expand to cover more positions (e.g. a hexad with accelerationism vs. degrowth)?
3. Are the dipole/triad assignments right, or do any of these want a different pole count?
4. Do the higher-pole candidates (platforms, tech ethics, AI risk positions) belong, or are they a different kind of thing?
5. The ground shifts fast here — is the stance framing (positions, not products) the right way to keep the taxonomy from dating itself, or does it need a different approach?
