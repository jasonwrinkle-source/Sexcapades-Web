## 1. Abstract & Scope

**Simulcapades** maps human positions on computational physics — the recognized "third pillar" of modern science standing between analytic theory and laboratory experiment. When differential equations cannot be solved in closed form and physical regimes are too extreme, too microscopic, or too vast to measure directly, the computer becomes a virtual laboratory.

In general tech discourse, scientific simulation is frequently confused with ordinary software development or cloud computing. In computational physics, however, code is an epistemic instrument designed to obey fundamental conservation laws, maintain symplectic phase space geometries, and resolve non-linear physics across orders of magnitude in length and time:
- Its core currency is the **physically verified, convergent simulation trajectory**.
- Its central conflicts revolve around **fidelity vs. speed, ab initio first principles vs. empirical surrogate potentials, numerical method selection, and the philosophical problem of simulation trust**.

Under the **Taxopolemics** engine, computational-physics positions are mapped to their natural geometric degrees of freedom: from **1-Pole scalars** (absolute affinity), to **2-Pole dialectical dipoles** (Fidelity vs. Speed; Deterministic vs. Stochastic), to higher-order **Triads (3-Pole $\Delta^2$)**, **Tetrads (4-Pole $\Delta^3$)**, **Pentads (5-Pole $\Delta^4$)**, **Hexads (6-Pole $\Delta^5$)**, **Heptads (7-Pole $\Delta^6$)**, and **Octads (8-Pole $\Delta^7$)**.

**Engine cap:** the engine goes to **8 poles and caps there** — monopole through octad. The cap is a human limit, not a technical one: people cannot hold more than about eight positions in mind at once (Jason, 2026-11-14).

**Scope boundaries:** Simulcapades is about computational physics algorithms, simulation trust, and physical modeling. It brushes **Techcapades** on high-performance computing (HPC) and hardware architectures, but focuses strictly on physics simulations rather than consumer software, cloud services, or corporate IT. It is distinct from **Quantacapades** (pen-and-paper theory) and **Expericapades** (laboratory hardware and real-world detectors).

**Name note:** "Simulcapades" from *simulation* — the computer as a virtual laboratory, the third pillar. The name was accepted by the Principal Investigator (2026-11-15) alongside Quantacapades (theoretical), Expericapades (experimental), and Mattercapades (applied).

**Synthesis note:** This briefing merges YoBiche's independent version (broader, more attitudinal — the life, the attitudes, the trust questions) with Antigravity's independent version (deeper, more technically specific — named methods, hardware, architectures). Neither version alone is complete; together they are strong. Where both sources cover the same topic, the better version was kept, as noted per topic. Two listed items were merged rather than counted twice: the Three Pillars triad and the Epistemic Status triad are the same question (1.1), and Accuracy vs Speed folds into Fidelity vs Speed (1.4) — so the approved review's 31 listed items become **29 topics**. ## 2. Taxonomic Architecture & Rating Widget Assignments

### Category 1: The Epistemology of Simulation

#### 1.1 The Three Pillars: What Is a Simulation? (3-Pole Triad $\Delta^2$)
*What is a computer simulation in the philosophy of science — and where does it stand among the three pillars?*
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Apex - Blue #3b82f6)`: **Virtual Experiment** — A simulation is a synthetic laboratory; you set up the physical environment, run the code, and observe emergent data like an experimentalist.
  - `Pole 1 (Base Right - Green #22c55e)`: **Extended Theory / Numerical Calculation** — A simulation is simply an automated numerical evaluator of existing analytic theory; it contains no new physics beyond the input equations.
  - `Pole 2 (Base Left - Purple #8b5cf6)`: **Autonomous Instrument** — Simulation is an entirely distinct third mode of knowledge creation that accesses complex, multi-scale emergent phenomena unreachable by either pure mathematics or laboratory instruments.
- **Question:** What is a simulation — a kind of theory, a kind of experiment, or a third pillar of its own?
- **Optimal Widget:** Barycentric Equilateral Triangle Widget (`"triangle"`).
- **Source:** Merged — Antigravity 1.1 (the pole framing) + YoBiche (the three-pillars framing: Theory / Experiment / Simulation). The two listed items are the same question: Theory ↔ Extended Theory, Experiment ↔ Virtual Experiment, Simulation as its own pillar ↔ Autonomous Instrument.

#### 1.2 Discovery vs Verification (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Discovery** (Simulation as a tool for finding new physics — exploring parameter space, finding emergent phenomena, predicting what no one has seen) ↔ **Verification** (Simulation as a tool for checking what you already believe — confirming analytic predictions, testing a theory against its consequences)
- **Question:** Do you simulate to discover new physics, or to verify what you already think is true?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 1.3 The Computational Experiment: Real vs Metaphor (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Real** (A simulation IS an experiment — it has a setup, a run, data, and error bars, and the results count as evidence in the same way lab data does) ↔ **Metaphor** (Calling it an "experiment" is just a metaphor — it is a calculation, and its results are only as good as the equations you fed in)
- **Question:** Is a simulation a real experiment, or is "experiment" just a metaphor for a calculation?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 1.4 Fidelity vs Speed: The Central Simulation Dilemma (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Maximum Physical Fidelity** (Double/quad precision, all electron shells, full quantum electrodynamics, zero empirical fudge factors; willing to wait 6 months on a supercomputer) ↔ **High-Throughput Speed & Scalability** (Coarse-grained approximations, GPU parallelism, real-time interactive parameter exploration; "good enough" physics at 100x the speed)
- **Question:** Do you prioritize uncompromising physical fidelity or high-throughput computational speed?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 1.2 + YoBiche. Absorbs the "Accuracy vs Speed" listing: accuracy and fidelity are the same pole here, so one topic, not two.

#### 1.5 Validation: Comparison to Physical Experiment vs. Mathematical Verification (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Empirical Validation First** (A simulation is meaningless unless its output curves match physical laboratory measurements within experimental error bars) ↔ **Internal Code Verification First** (The supreme test is mathematical: proving grid convergence, asymptotic order of accuracy, and exact conservation of energy, momentum, and symplectic structure)
- **Question:** Is a simulation validated by matching lab data or by mathematical convergence?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 1.3 + YoBiche.

#### 1.6 The Simulation Trust Paradox: "Nobody Believes the Simulation" (2-Pole Dipole)
*Reflecting the famous lab proverb: "Everyone believes the experiment except the experimentalist; nobody believes the simulation except the simulator."*
- **Poles:** **Bold Simulation Confidence** (Trust the computational output as ground truth; modern simulations are often cleaner and less prone to systematic errors than real-world noise) ↔ **Permanent Self-Skepticism** (Never trust a simulation fully; discretization errors, floating-point roundoff, and unmodeled boundary artifacts are always lurking)
- **Question:** How much inherent trust do you place in simulation results?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 1.4.

### Category 2: Methods

#### 2.1 The Computational Physics Methods Quartet (4-Pole Tetrad $\Delta^3$)
*The four dominant algorithmic paradigms for simulating physical matter and fields.*
- **Poles:**
  - `North (Blue #3b82f6)`: **Electronic Structure & Ab Initio Quantum** — Density Functional Theory (DFT), Quantum Monte Carlo, Hartree-Fock, coupled-cluster methods for electrons and orbitals.
  - `East (Emerald #10b981)`: **Classical & Molecular Dynamics (MD)** — Newtonian particle equations of motion, force fields, Lennard-Jones, N-body astrophysics, coarse-grained biomolecules.
  - `South (Red #ef4444)`: **Mesh & Continuum Solvers** — Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), Finite Element Analysis (FEA), Particle-In-Cell (PIC) for plasmas.
  - `West (Amber #f59e0b)`: **Statistical & Stochastic Monte Carlo** — Lattice gauge theory, Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), cluster algorithms (Wolff, Swendsen-Wang), simulated annealing.
- **Question:** Which computational physics methodology is your primary weapon?
- **Optimal Widget:** Orthogonal Diamond Deformation Polygon (`"diamond"`).
- **Source:** Antigravity 2.1 — the tetrad adds Mesh & Continuum Solvers as a fourth pole over YoBiche's triad.

#### 2.2 First-Principles vs Empirical (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Ab Initio Quantum Purity (DFT)** (Calculate electronic forces directly from Schrödinger/Kohn-Sham equations; zero fitted parameters; limited to thousands of atoms) ↔ **Empirical Classical Potentials** (Embedded Atom Method, Tersoff, ReaxFF; fitted to bulk properties; capable of simulating billions of atoms)
- **Question:** Do you calculate forces from first principles or fit empirical potential functions?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 2.2 + YoBiche.

#### 2.3 Classical vs Quantum Simulators (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Classical Supercomputing Algorithms** (Tensor networks, DMRG, Quantum Monte Carlo on classical exascale clusters) ↔ **Quantum Hardware / Analog Simulators** (Mapping the Hamiltonian directly onto neutral atom arrays, superconducting qubits, or Rydberg quantum simulators)
- **Question:** Will strongly correlated quantum systems be solved by classical algorithms or quantum hardware?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 2.3.

#### 2.4 Continuum vs Discrete (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Continuum** (Fields, PDEs, smooth functions — the world is continuous and the math is calculus) ↔ **Discrete** (Particles, lattices, graphs — the world is granular and the math is counting)
- **Question:** Is the world continuous or discrete — and does your simulation treat it that way?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 2.5 Deterministic vs Stochastic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Deterministic** (Same input, same output, every time — the physics is a clockwork and the simulation should be too) ↔ **Stochastic** (Randomness is in the physics — Monte Carlo, thermal fluctuations, quantum measurement — and the simulation must sample, not solve)
- **Question:** Is your simulation a clockwork or a dice roll?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

### Category 3: Scale, Discretization & Time

#### 3.1 Multi-Scale Bridging: Concurrent vs Hierarchical (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Direct Concurrent Multi-Scale** (Simultaneously embedding quantum QM/MM zones inside classical MD, coupled directly to continuum finite element boundaries in a single code run) ↔ **Hierarchical Parameter Hand-Off** (Run quantum simulations offline to compute material constants, pass them up to molecular dynamics, extract viscosity/elasticity, and feed them into a separate macro CFD code)
- **Question:** Do you couple multi-scale models concurrently or pass parameters hierarchically?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 3.1.

#### 3.2 Symplectic vs Adaptive Steppers (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Symplectic Integrators (Geometric Mechanics)** (Verlet, leapfrog, Ruth algorithms; exact conservation of phase space volume and bounded Hamiltonian energy over billions of orbits) ↔ **High-Order Adaptive Steppers** (Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg, Bulirsch-Stoer; adaptive time steps that minimize local truncation error at every moment)
- **Question:** Do you prioritize symplectic geometric conservation or high-order adaptive accuracy?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 3.2.

#### 3.3 Spatial Discretization: Structured Grids vs. AMR vs. Mesh-Free (3-Pole Triad $\Delta^2$)
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Apex - Blue #3b82f6)`: **Structured Uniform Grids & Spectral Methods** — Cartesian grids, Fast Fourier Transforms, uniform spacing; maximum computational efficiency and spectral accuracy.
  - `Pole 1 (Base Right - Green #22c55e)`: **Unstructured Adaptive Meshes (AMR)** — Tetrahedral/hexahedral meshes, octrees, zooming in on shock fronts and boundary layers where physics happens.
  - `Pole 2 (Base Left - Amber #f59e0b)`: **Mesh-Free Particle Methods** — Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH), vortex methods; no mesh connectivity, immune to mesh tangling during explosive deformation.
- **Question:** How do you discretize physical space?
- **Optimal Widget:** Barycentric Equilateral Triangle Widget (`"triangle"`).
- **Source:** Antigravity 3.3.

#### 3.4 Resolution vs Domain Size (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Resolution** (Fine grids, small timesteps, resolve every detail — the physics is in the fine structure) ↔ **Domain Size** (Big boxes, long times, the whole system — the physics is in the large-scale behavior)
- **Question:** Do you resolve the fine detail or cover the whole system?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 3.5 Convergence: Strict vs Pragmatic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Strict** (Prove convergence — grid refinement studies, Richardson extrapolation, error bounds; no result counts until the convergence is demonstrated) ↔ **Pragmatic** (If it looks converged, it is converged — run it twice at different resolutions, check the answer doesn't move much, and get on with the physics)
- **Question:** Do you prove convergence, or just check it looks right?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

### Category 4: The Machine & The Code

#### 4.1 HPC vs Hardware You Control (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **National Leadership Supercomputers** (Frontier, Aurora, Summit; 100,000+ nodes, massive MPI scaling, rigorous grant allocation proposals and queue waits) ↔ **Hardware You Control** (Local dedicated workstations or cloud instances; instant turnaround, interactive debugging, zero grant bureaucracy — or pay-as-you-go cloud)
- **Question:** Do you run on national supercomputers or on hardware you control?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 4.1 (HPC vs local workstations) + YoBiche (HPC vs Cloud). The cloud pole extends her local-workstation pole: the question is who controls the machine, not just where it sits.

#### 4.2 Processor Architecture: CPU vs GPU vs Quantum (3-Pole Triad $\Delta^2$)
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Apex - Blue #3b82f6)`: **Massive CPU Cores** — Huge RAM per core, deep cache hierarchies, complex branch prediction, pure OpenMP/MPI parallelism.
  - `Pole 1 (Base Right - Green #22c55e)`: **Dense GPU / Tensor Accelerators** — Thousands of CUDA/HIP cores, massive memory bandwidth, SIMD lockstep parallelism, porting physics to GPU kernels.
  - `Pole 2 (Base Left - Purple #8b5cf6)`: **Quantum Processors** — The computation itself is quantum; the hardware is the physics, not just a faster way to do classical arithmetic.
- **Question:** Is your physics algorithm better suited for high-memory CPUs, dense streaming GPUs, or quantum hardware?
- **Optimal Widget:** Barycentric Equilateral Triangle Widget (`"triangle"`).
- **Source:** Merged — Antigravity 4.2 (CPU vs GPU dipole) + YoBiche (adds Quantum as a third pole). The quantum pole is not just a faster processor — it changes what computation means.

#### 4.3 Language: Performance vs Productivity (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Battle-Tested Fortran/C Codebases** (Millions of lines written over 40 years, hyper-optimized BLAS/LAPACK, reliable, immovable institutional code) ↔ **Modern Composable High-Level Stacks** (Julia, Python/JAX, differentiable physics engines, auto-differentiation, high-level expressive abstractions)
- **Question:** Do you build upon 40-year-old Fortran monoliths or modern modular scientific languages?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 4.3 + YoBiche. The Fortran-vs-modern divide is the performance-vs-productivity divide in practice.

#### 4.4 PINNs vs Traditional Solvers (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **AI & Neural Operators (PINNs/FNO)** (Train neural networks on physics loss functions; instantaneous surrogate inference, mesh-free, solves inverse problems effortlessly) ↔ **Traditional Numerical Analysis (FEM/FVM)** (Strictly mathematically verified error bounds, exact conservation laws, no hallucinated physics, guaranteed stability)
- **Question:** Will deep learning neural operators replace traditional numerical solvers?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 5.1.

#### 4.5 MLIPs — Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **MLIP Enthusiast** (Neural network potentials like MACE, NequIP, and ANI achieve near-DFT quantum accuracy at classical molecular dynamics speed; the holy grail is here) ↔ **MLIP Skeptic** (Machine learning potentials fail unpredictably outside their training manifold; dangerous and untrustworthy for unexpected phase transitions or extreme pressures)
- **Question:** Do you trust machine-learned interatomic potentials for new physics discovery?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 5.2.

### Category 5: Trust, Error & Reproducibility

#### 5.1 Error Bars: Honest vs Optimistic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Honest** (Report the full error — systematic and statistical, all of it, even when it makes the result look weak) ↔ **Optimistic** (Report the error that makes the result look good — the statistical error only, or the best-case systematics, because the paper needs a clean number)
- **Question:** Do your error bars tell the truth, or tell the story you want?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 5.2 Reproducibility: Code vs Results (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Code** (Reproducibility means the code — publish the source, the input decks, the random seeds, and anyone can re-run it) ↔ **Results** (Reproducibility means the results — an independent group with different code should get the same physics, and re-running your code only proves your code runs)
- **Question:** Does reproducibility mean sharing your code, or getting the same answer independently?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 5.3 Black Box: Trust vs Verify (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Trust** (Use the library, the package, the solver — it works, the authors know what they're doing, and your job is the physics, not the numerics) ↔ **Verify** (Never trust a black box — read the source, test it against known solutions, understand what it does before you let it touch your physics)
- **Question:** Do you trust the solver, or do you verify it first?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 5.4 Code Openness (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Radical Open-Source Science** (Publishing all solver code, input decks, random seeds, and raw trajectory data on GitHub/Zenodo with Docker containers for 100% reproduction) ↔ **Protected Competitive Advantage** (Keeping custom in-house physics algorithms proprietary until all papers and grants are extracted)
- **Question:** Should simulation source code and input decks be fully open upon publication?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Overlap — Antigravity 6.1 + YoBiche.

### Category 6: The Computational Life

#### 6.1 Legacy Code: Maintain vs Rewrite (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Maintain** (The old code works — it has been validated for decades, the bugs are known, and rewriting risks losing physics that nobody remembers is in there) ↔ **Rewrite** (The old code is unmaintainable — spaghetti Fortran, no tests, no documentation, and the only way forward is to start clean)
- **Question:** Do you maintain the legacy code or rewrite it?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 6.2 Specialist vs Generalist (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Specialist** (One method, deeply — you are the DFT person, or the Monte Carlo person, and your depth is your value) ↔ **Generalist** (Many methods, broadly — you pick the right tool for each problem, and your range is your value)
- **Question:** Are you a specialist in one method or a generalist across many?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 6.3 Theory vs Implementation (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Theory** (The physics comes first — the equations, the model, the understanding; the code is just how you evaluate it) ↔ **Implementation** (The code comes first — the algorithm, the data structures, the performance; a theory you can't compute is a wish, not a result)
- **Question:** Do you start from the theory or from the implementation?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** YoBiche.

#### 6.4 Physicist vs Research Software Engineer (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** **Physicist First** (Code is just an incidental vehicle to understand the physics; quick-and-dirty scripts are fine if the physical insight is sound) ↔ **Research Software Engineer (RSE)** (Code quality, unit testing, continuous integration, version control, and memory safety are as crucial as the physical equations)
- **Question:** Do you view yourself primarily as a physicist who codes, or as a scientific software engineer?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole Slider.
- **Source:** Antigravity 6.2.
