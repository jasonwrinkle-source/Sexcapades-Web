## 1. Abstract & Scope

**Modelcapades** maps human positions on applied mathematics — how you model the world, how you compute, what you value in applied work, where you apply it, how you handle uncertainty, and how you live as an applied mathematician. In traditional surveys, applied math is collapsed into crude single-axis binaries (e.g. Theory vs. Practice) or reduced to a list of applications.

Under the **Taxopolemics** engine, applied-mathematical positions are mapped to their natural geometric degrees of freedom: from **1-Pole scalars** (absolute affinity), to **2-Pole dialectical dipoles** (Deterministic vs. Stochastic; Analytic vs. Numerical), to higher-order **Triads (3-Pole $\Delta^2$)**, **Tetrads (4-Pole $\Delta^3$)**, **Pentads (5-Pole $\Delta^4$)**, **Hexads (6-Pole $\Delta^5$)**, **Heptads (7-Pole $\Delta^6$)**, and **Octads (8-Pole $\Delta^7$)**.

**Engine cap:** the engine goes to **8 poles and caps there** — monopole through octad. The cap is a human limit, not a technical one: people cannot hold more than about eight positions in mind at once (Jason, 2026-11-14).

**Scope note:** Modelcapades is the tenth skin, and the second of the math pair. It covers applied mathematics. Pure and theoretical mathematics is a separate skin (Abstracapades) — the two are different cultures with different values, and a single skin would dilute both. The pure/applied divide is itself a topic in this skin (4.2). Modelcapades brushes Techcapades (computation, data, AI) and Vocapades (engineering careers, industry vs academia); this skin goes deeper into the specifically mathematical positions — how you model, how you solve, how you handle uncertainty. The audience is narrower than the other skins — applied mathematicians, engineers, data scientists, and math-adjacent people — but the positions are real and passionate, and for collaborators they determine whether you can work together.

**Name note:** The skin was first drafted as "Appliedcapades" (2026-11-15). The Principal Investigator hated that name. He briefly accepted "Applied-Mathcapades," then changed his mind on the pure skin's name (Abstracapades). For the applied skin, he accepted **Modelcapades** tentatively ("tentative Modelcapades... go ahead") — modeling is the core of applied math. The name may change.

## 2. Taxonomic Architecture & Rating Widget Assignments

### Category 1: Modeling the World

#### 1.1 Deterministic vs Stochastic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Deterministic (the world is predictable; given the inputs, the outputs follow) ↔ Stochastic (the world is random; you model distributions, not certainties)
- **Question:** Is the world predictable or random?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 1.2 Continuous vs Discrete Models (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Continuous (differential equations, flows, fields) ↔ Discrete (difference equations, networks, agents)
- **Question:** Do you model with continuous flows or discrete systems?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 1.3 First Principles vs Empirical (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** First Principles (derive from theory; the model comes from the physics) ↔ Empirical (fit to data; the model comes from observation)
- **Question:** Do you derive from theory or fit to data?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 2: Computation & Method

#### 2.1 Analytic vs Numerical (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Analytic (solve exactly; closed form, pen and paper) ↔ Numerical (approximate; algorithms, computers, floating point)
- **Question:** Do you solve exactly or approximate?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 2.2 Simulation vs Theory (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Simulation (run it and see; Monte Carlo, agent-based, computational experiments) ↔ Theory (prove it; the math tells you what happens)
- **Question:** Do you trust simulations or theory?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 2.3 Optimization: Exact vs Heuristic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Exact (find the true optimum; global, guaranteed) ↔ Heuristic (find a good-enough solution; fast, practical, approximate)
- **Question:** Do you find the optimum or a good-enough answer?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 3: Rigor & Utility

#### 3.1 Rigor vs Utility (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Rigor (applied math needs proof; the foundations matter) ↔ Utility (if it works, it works; the result matters, not the proof)
- **Question:** Does applied math need to be rigorous, or just work?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 3.2 Elegance vs Effectiveness (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Elegance (a beautiful model; clean, spare, insightful) ↔ Effectiveness (an effective model; it predicts, it controls, it delivers)
- **Question:** Do you value a beautiful model or an effective one?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 3.3 General vs Specific (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** General (a theory that covers many problems; reusable, abstract) ↔ Specific (a solution tailored to one problem; bespoke, concrete)
- **Question:** Do you build general theories or problem-specific solutions?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 4: Domains & Applications

#### 4.1 Physics vs Biology vs Finance vs Engineering (4-Pole Tetrad $\Delta^3$)
- **Poles:**
  - `North (Blue #3b82f6)`: **Physics** — Mechanics, fields, waves, the physical world.
  - `East (Green #22c55e)`: **Biology** — Populations, epidemics, ecosystems, the living world.
  - `South (Yellow #eab308)`: **Finance** — Markets, risk, options, the economic world.
  - `West (Orange #f97316)`: **Engineering** — Structures, control, signals, the built world.
- **Question:** Where do you apply math?
- **Optimal Widget:** Orthogonal Diamond Deformation Polygon.

#### 4.2 Pure vs Applied (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Pure (math for its own sake) ↔ Applied (math for what it does)
- **Question:** The skin-defining divide — where do you stand, from the applied side?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 4.3 Interdisciplinary vs Domain-Specific (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Interdisciplinary (work across fields; borrow methods, cross boundaries) ↔ Domain-Specific (deep in one field; master its methods, its literature, its problems)
- **Question:** Do you work across fields or deep in one?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 5: Data & Uncertainty

#### 5.1 Bayesian vs Frequentist (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Bayesian (probability is belief; update with evidence; priors matter) ↔ Frequentist (probability is frequency; long-run rates; objectivity matters)
- **Question:** How do you handle probability?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 5.2 Big Data vs Small Data (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Big Data (more data; let the data speak; machine learning, scale) ↔ Small Data (better models; careful design; insight over volume)
- **Question:** More data or better models?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 5.3 Prediction vs Explanation (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Prediction (forecast the outcome; accuracy is what counts) ↔ Explanation (understand the mechanism; insight is what counts)
- **Question:** Do you predict outcomes or explain mechanisms?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 6: The Applied Life

#### 6.1 Industry vs Academia (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Industry (real problems, real deadlines, real impact) ↔ Academia (open problems, peer review, the literature)
- **Question:** Where do you do applied math?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 6.2 Collaboration vs Solitude (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Collaboration (work with domain experts; the team solves it) ↔ Solitude (work alone; the math is yours)
- **Question:** Do you work with domain experts or alone?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 6.3 Impact vs Publication (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Impact (real-world change; the model ships, the system improves) ↔ Publication (academic papers; the literature, the citations, the record)
- **Question:** Do you value real-world impact or academic publication?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.
