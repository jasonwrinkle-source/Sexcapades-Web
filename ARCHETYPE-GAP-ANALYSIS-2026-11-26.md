## NOUS (51 topics, 7 archetypes) — 55 / 50 / 5 gaps; 3 misstatements

Archetypes: Computationalist, Connector, Embodied Biopsychist, Levin Supplement (Embodied Biopsychist + Quantum Mind), Pluralist, plus two others (7 total). 9 topics show no strong position (≤ 25) from any archetype — these are dead ground, not gaps: Cognition without a brain? no — that one splits; the flat nine are those with no archetype taking a strong position.

### Gaps

**N1 — Qualia, real or illusion?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Computationalist (functionalism) holds qualia reducible / epiphenomenal; the Levin Supplement (biopsychist + quantum mind) holds qualia the real irreducible stuff; the Embodied Biopsychist agrees qualia are real but as *bodily* feeling. The topic "Qualia" does not exist anywhere in the taxonomy — no topic asks whether phenomenal experience is real or illusory. Poles: *Real and irreducible / real but embodied / epiphenomenal or reducible*. Plain language: "Is the 'what it feels like' of experience real and fundamental, or just what computation looks like from the inside?"

**N2 — Does cognition need feeling?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Levin Supplement holds cognition *requires* feeling (affective, embodied); the Computationalist holds cognition without feeling is possible (a system that thinks without feeling); the Pluralist sits between. This is the Levin-vs-computation fault line, and it is also where the "cognition without a brain" misstatement lives — the taxonomy conflates "brain" with "feeling". Poles: *Feeling is required / cognition is separate from feeling / feeling is epiphenomenal*. Plain language: "Can something think without feeling anything — or is feeling part of what thinking is?"

**N3 — The combination problem** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Pluralist (panpsychist) holds micro-experiences combine into macro-consciousness; the Computationalist (functionalist) holds combination is a non-issue (functional roles, not aggregation); the Embodied Biopsychist holds combination is real but through *bodies*, not through micro-minds. The taxonomy asks "Panpsychism" (2 poles) but never asks *how* micro-experiences combine — the combination problem is the central objection to panpsychism and it is unasked. Poles: *Combination is real and explained / combination is a hard problem, unsolved / combination is a non-issue (functional roles)*. Plain language: "If tiny bits of experience exist, how do they add up to one mind — or do they?"

**N4 — Free will** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Computationalist (compatibilist / determinist) holds free will is compatible with determinism (or an illusion); the Levin Supplement (biopsychist + quantum mind) holds free will is real and grounded in quantum indeterminacy / embodied choice; the Embodied Biopsychist holds free will is real but as *bodily* agency. The taxonomy has no free-will topic anywhere — not in Nous, not in Philo (Philo's gap P5 is the same hole, seen from the other side). Poles: *Real and grounded in indeterminacy / compatible with determinism / an illusion*. Plain language: "Is free will real — and if so, does it need quantum randomness, or just the right kind of determinism?"

**N5 — Moratorium pole missing on Ethics of Synthetic Morphology** — MISSING POLE, **Ethics of synthetic morphology** (depth 2, switchbox → trilinear).
Disagreement: the Levin Supplement (biopsychist + quantum mind) holds a *moratorium* on synthetic morphology (a pause, a "not yet") — but the topic offers only "Permit" vs "Prohibit". The moratorium position (pause until we know more) is a distinct stance that neither pole expresses. The Levin Supplement rates Permit −50 / Prohibit +50, which misreads as "prohibit" when the literature says "pause". Poles: add *Moratorium (pause until we know more)* as a third pole. Plain language: "Should we pause synthetic-morphology research until we understand it better — neither permitting nor banning it outright?"

### Misstatements (3)

**M1 — Embodied Biopsychist on "Cognition without a brain": "Needs a brain" +70.** The Embodied Biopsychist holds life–mind continuity (cognition is embodied, but not necessarily *brained* — Levin's work is about cognition in cells and tissues without brains). Rating "Needs a brain" +70 makes it look like a brain chauvinist, which contradicts the life–mind continuity position. It should be closer to "Needs a body / needs embodiment" — the pole framing conflates brain with body. Fix: reframe the pole or move the rating toward the embodied pole.

**M2 — Pluralist + Computationalist on "Platonic Space of Patterns": "Non-Physical Form Attractors" +72 / +62.** Both the Pluralist and the Computationalist are physicalists (the Pluralist is a panpsychist physicalist; the Computationalist is a functionalist physicalist). Rating "Non-Physical Form Attractors" +72/+62 makes them look like Platonists who believe in non-physical forms, which contradicts their physicalism. They should be on the physical pole (patterns as physical regularities, not non-physical forms). Fix: move both to the physical pole, or reframe the pole to distinguish "patterns as real" from "patterns as non-physical".

**M3 — Computationalist on "What selfhood feels like": "The hard problem, reframed" +68.** The Computationalist holds that selfhood *dissolves* under functionalism (the self is a functional role, not a thing that feels like something). Rating "The hard problem, reframed" +68 makes it look like the Computationalist takes the hard problem seriously as a problem, when the position is that the hard problem dissolves. It should be on the dissolution pole. Fix: move to the dissolution pole.

### Structural note
Two topics share the name "The Cognitive Light Cone" — a naming collision; one should be renamed.

### Biggest gap: **N1 — Qualia, real or illusion?** The skin's central question (what experience *is*) is never asked.

## QUANTA (30 topics, 6 archetypes) — 16 / 13 / 3 gaps; 3 misstatements

Archetypes: 4 originals (unrated on 5 topics) + 2 new (GRW, QBism — both rated on all topics). The 4 originals are unrated on 5 topics — rating gaps, not taxonomy gaps.

### Gaps

**Q1 — What counts as an observer?** — NEW TOPIC, diamond, **Foundations** (depth 2).
Disagreement: Copenhagen says a measurement apparatus / classical observer collapses the wavefunction; Many-Worlds says there is no collapse, only branching (no privileged observer); QBism says the observer is the *agent* (a participatory, Bayesian agent); RQM says any physical system can be an observer (relational). The taxonomy asks "Quantum Interpretations" (a diamond) but never asks *what an observer is* — and the interpretations differ precisely on this. Poles (diamond): *Classical apparatus / any physical system / the agent / no observer (branching)*. Plain language: "What counts as an observer — a machine, any physical thing, a conscious agent, or nobody at all?"

**Q2 — Where does quantum probability come from?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Foundations** (depth 2).
Disagreement: Copenhagen says probability is fundamental (Born rule, irreducible); Many-Worlds says probability is emergent (branching weights, decision-theoretic); QBism says probability is subjective (degrees of belief); GRW says probability is objective (spontaneous collapse rates). The taxonomy never asks where probability comes from — it asks about interpretations, but the *source* of probability is the fault line the interpretations disagree on. Poles: *Fundamental (Born rule) / emergent (branching) / subjective (belief) / objective (collapse)*. Plain language: "Is quantum probability a law of nature, a fact about branching, a measure of belief, or a collapse rate?"

**Q3 — Can your interpretation be tested?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Foundations** (depth 2).
Disagreement: GRW says yes (spontaneous collapse is testable — experiments can bound the collapse rate); Many-Worlds says no in principle (branching is unobservable); QBism says the question is misposed (interpretations are frameworks, not theories); Copenhagen says no (the interpretation is a rulebook, not a prediction). The taxonomy never asks whether an interpretation is testable — and GRW's entire *raison d'être* is testability. Poles: *Yes, testable (collapse bounds) / no in principle (branching) / the question is misposed*. Plain language: "Can we ever test which interpretation is right — or is the choice between them a matter of taste?"

### Misstatements (3)

**M1 — GRW has no pole on "Quantum Interpretations" (diamond).** All four poles rate negative for GRW, intensity −85. The diamond has 4 poles for 5 interpretations (Copenhagen, Many-Worlds, QBism, RQM) — GRW has no pole, so it reads as "GRW rejects all interpretations", which is false (GRW *is* an interpretation). Fix: split QBism + RQM (they currently share one pole) and add GRW as a 6th pole — the topic becomes a 6-pole star-radar.

**M2 — QBism + RQM share one pole on "Quantum Interpretations".** QBism (agent-centred, Bayesian) and RQM (relational, any system an observer) are distinct interpretations that disagree on what an observer is (see Q1). Sharing a pole makes them look identical. Fix: split them (see M1).

**M3 — Many-Worlds on Bell's theorem rates "Superdeterminism" +80.** Many-Worlds does not hold superdeterminism — it holds *local branching* (correlations arise from branching, not from superdeterminism or nonlocality). The topic is missing a "Local branching" pole, so Many-Worlds is forced onto Superdeterminism +80, which misstates its position. Fix: add a "Local branching" pole.

### Biggest gap: **Q1 — What counts as an observer?** The interpretations differ precisely on this, and it is never asked.

## BIO (41 topics, 5 archetypes) — 35 / 30 / 5 gaps; 0 misstatements

Archetypes: Gene-Centric Reductionist, Systems & Evo-Devo Holist, Bio-Engineering Promethean, plus 2 originals. No misstatements. 5 gaps, all new topics.

### Gaps

**B1 — Why does biodiversity matter?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Conservation & the value of life** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Gene-Centric Reductionist holds biodiversity matters instrumentally (genetic resources, ecosystem services); the Systems Holist holds it matters intrinsically (the web of life has value in itself); the Bio-Engineering Promethean holds it matters as *material* (a parts library for engineering). The taxonomy asks about conservation *actions* but never asks *why* biodiversity matters — and the archetypes split on the reason, not the action. Poles: *Instrumental (resources) / intrinsic (value in itself) / material (parts library)*. Plain language: "Is biodiversity worth protecting because it's useful, because it's valuable in itself, or because it's raw material for engineering?"

**B2 — What is a biological function?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Gene-Centric Reductionist holds function is selected effect (what a trait was selected *for*); the Systems Holist holds function is organizational (what a trait *does* in the system); the Bio-Engineering Promethean holds function is designable (what we can *make* it do). The taxonomy never asks what a function is — and the archetypes' whole disagreement about reduction vs holism turns on it. Poles: *Selected effect / organizational role / designable*. Plain language: "What makes something a function — that it was selected for, that it plays a role in a system, or that we can design it?"

**B3 — What is a species?** — NEW TOPIC, diamond, **Core commitments** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Gene-Centric Reductionist holds the biological species concept (reproductive isolation); the Systems Holist holds the ecological / phylogenetic concept (niche, lineage); the Bio-Engineering Promethean holds the concept is operational (whatever we can engineer around). The taxonomy never asks what a species is — and the species problem is the oldest fault line in biology. Poles (diamond): *Reproductive isolation / ecological niche / phylogenetic lineage / operational*. Plain language: "What makes two organisms the same species — that they can interbreed, that they share a niche, that they share a lineage, or that we can work with them the same way?"

**B4 — What should conservation protect?** — NEW TOPIC, triangle, **Conservation & the value of life** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Gene-Centric Reductionist holds conservation should protect *genes* (genetic diversity); the Systems Holist holds it should protect *ecosystems* (processes, webs); the Bio-Engineering Promethean holds it should protect *capacities* (the ability to engineer, to adapt). The taxonomy asks about conservation actions but never asks *what* conservation should protect — and the archetypes split on the target. Poles: *Genes / ecosystems / capacities*. Plain language: "Should conservation protect genes, whole ecosystems, or the capacity of life to adapt and be engineered?"

**B5 — Is evolution going anywhere?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Core commitments** (depth 2, weakest gap).
Disagreement: the Gene-Centric Reductionist holds evolution has no direction (drift + selection, no telos); the Systems Holist holds evolution has a direction (increasing complexity, integration); the Bio-Engineering Promethean holds evolution is *steerable* (we can give it a direction). The taxonomy never asks whether evolution has a direction. Poles: *No direction / increasing complexity / steerable*. Plain language: "Is evolution heading somewhere — or is it just drift and selection with no destination?"

### Biggest gap: **B1 — Why does biodiversity matter?** The archetypes agree on conservation actions but split on the reason, and the reason is never asked.

## THEO (31 topics, 6 archetypes) — 17 / 14 / 3 gaps; 1 misstatement

Archetypes: 4 originals (unrated on 3 topics) + 2 new (both rated on all topics).

### Gaps

**T1 — Can mathematics be revised?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Foundations of mathematics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Formalist holds mathematics is revisable (axioms are conventions, we can change them); the Platonist holds mathematics is *not* revisable (mathematical truths are discovered, not invented); the Structuralist holds mathematics is revisable *in form* but not in structure (the structures are real, the presentations are conventional). The taxonomy asks about foundations but never asks whether mathematics itself can be revised — and the archetypes split on it. Poles: *Revisable (conventions) / not revisable (discovered truths) / revisable in form, not structure*. Plain language: "Can we change mathematics — or do we just discover it?"

**T2 — How do we know mathematical truths?** — NEW TOPIC, diamond, **Foundations of mathematics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Platonist holds we know mathematical truths by *intuition* (direct apprehension of abstract objects); the Formalist holds we know them by *proof* (derivation from axioms); the Structuralist holds we know them by *pattern-recognition* (grasping structures). The taxonomy never asks how mathematical knowledge works — and the epistemology of mathematics is the fault line the foundations debate turns on. Poles (diamond): *Intuition / proof / pattern-recognition / construction*. Plain language: "How do we know a mathematical truth — by seeing it, by proving it, by recognizing a pattern, or by building it?"

**T3 — One true mathematics or many?** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Foundations of mathematics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Platonist holds there is one true mathematics (one universe of mathematical objects); the Formalist holds there are many (different axiom systems, different mathematics); the Structuralist holds there is one *structure* with many presentations. The taxonomy never asks whether mathematics is one or many. Poles: *One true mathematics / many mathematics / one structure, many presentations*. Plain language: "Is there one mathematics, or many — and if many, are they all equally real?"

### Misstatement (1)

**M1 — "Foundations of Mathematics" star-radar has 5 poles for 6 schools; no Quine–Putnam pole.** The Quine–Putnam indispensability argument (mathematics is true because it is indispensable to science) has no pole, so the Quine–Putnam position is forced onto Structuralism +60, which misstates it (Quine–Putnam is not structuralism — it is a naturalistic argument for mathematical realism). Fix: add a Quine–Putnam pole (6th pole).

### Biggest gap: **T1 — Can mathematics be revised?** The foundations debate turns on it and it is never asked.

## PHILO (23 topics, 7 archetypes) — 29 / 21 / 8 gaps; 2 misstatements; 13 unrated cells

Archetypes: 7 (all rated on most topics; 13 unrated cells across the set).

### Gaps

**P1 — God & the Supernatural** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Metaphysics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: splits all seven archetypes — the theist holds God exists; the atheist holds God does not exist; the agnostic holds we cannot know; the pantheist holds God *is* nature; the deist holds God exists but does not intervene. The taxonomy has no God topic anywhere — the oldest fault line in philosophy is unasked. Poles: *Theist / atheist / agnostic / pantheist / deist*. Plain language: "Does God exist — and if so, what kind of God?"

**P2 — Causation** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Metaphysics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the Humean holds causation is regular succession (no necessary connection); the realist holds causation is a real relation (powers, mechanisms); the interventionist holds causation is manipulability (what we can change). The taxonomy never asks what causation is. Poles: *Regular succession / real relation (powers) / manipulability*. Plain language: "What is causation — a habit of seeing one thing follow another, a real power in things, or what we can intervene on?"

**P3 — Personal Identity** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Metaphysics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the psychological-continuity theorist holds identity is continuity of memory and character; the bodily theorist holds identity is continuity of the body; the no-self theorist holds there is no persisting self at all. The taxonomy never asks what makes a person the same person over time. Poles: *Psychological continuity / bodily continuity / no persisting self*. Plain language: "What makes you the same person you were ten years ago — your memories, your body, or nothing at all?"

**P4 — Scientific Realism** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Epistemology** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the realist holds science describes reality (theories are approximately true); the instrumentalist holds science is a tool (theories are useful fictions); the structural realist holds science describes *structure*, not objects. The taxonomy never asks whether science describes reality. Poles: *Realist (theories true) / instrumentalist (theories useful) / structural realist (structures real)*. Plain language: "Does science tell us what the world is really like — or just give us useful tools for predicting it?"

**P5 — Objective Meaning of Life** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Ethics** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the objectivist holds life has objective meaning (there are facts about what makes life meaningful); the subjectivist holds meaning is created (each person makes their own); the nihilist holds there is no meaning. The taxonomy never asks whether life has objective meaning. Poles: *Objective meaning / created meaning / no meaning*. Plain language: "Is there a fact about what makes life meaningful — or do we each make our own meaning, or is there none?"

**P6 — Political Legitimacy** — NEW TOPIC, diamond, **Political philosophy** (depth 2).
Disagreement: the consent theorist holds legitimacy comes from consent; the consequentialist holds it comes from outcomes (good government); the proceduralist holds it comes from fair procedures; the anarchist holds no state is legitimate. The taxonomy never asks what makes a government legitimate. Poles (diamond): *Consent / outcomes / procedures / no state legitimate*. Plain language: "What makes a government legitimate — that we agreed to it, that it governs well, that it follows fair rules, or nothing at all?"

**P7 — Modal Realism** — NEW TOPIC, trilinear-line, **Metaphysics** (depth 2, thinner gap).
Disagreement: the modal realist (Lewis) holds possible worlds are real, concrete places; the actualist holds only the actual world is real (possibilities are ways of talking); the ersatzist holds possible worlds are abstract representations. The taxonomy never asks whether possible worlds are real. Poles: *Real concrete worlds / only the actual world / abstract representations*. Plain language: "Are there really other possible worlds out there — or is 'possible' just a way of talking?"

**P8 — Neutral-monism pole missing on Mind–Body Ontology Matrix** — MISSING POLE, **Mind–body** (depth 2, switchbox → triangle).
Disagreement: the neutral monist holds mind and body are both aspects of a neutral substance (neither mental nor physical) — but the topic offers only "Physicalism" vs "Dualism". Neutral monism is a distinct position that neither pole expresses. Poles: add *Neutral monism* as a third pole.
