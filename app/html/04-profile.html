Gender
Choose up to three gender identities. Used for gender language in scene prompts; it does not assume anatomy. Scene prompts use your Pronouns field if you set one, and otherwise go by a single clear gender here.Choose at least one gender.
PronounsWhat you write here is what the story and scene writers use, exactly as written. Leave it blank to go by your gender instead: he/him when every gender you picked is on the man’s side (Man, Trans Man, Intersex Man), she/her when every one is on the woman’s side (Woman, Trans Woman, Intersex Woman), and they/them for anything else — a mix of sides, any other gender, or none picked. Pick a preset or type your own.
He/Him — Used for men; some nonbinary people also use he/him.
She/Her — Used for women; some nonbinary people also use she/her.
They/Them — Gender-neutral singular pronouns used in English for centuries; the most common choice among nonbinary people in the surveys cited by Nonbinary Wiki.
Ze/Hir — A gender-neutral neopronoun set (ze, hir, hir, hirs, hirself); the best-attested of the ze sets, used by Leslie Feinberg.
Ze/Zir — A ze-family variant (ze, zir, zir, zirs, zirself), documented in internet use since at least the 1990s.
Xe/Xem — A gender-neutral set (xe, xem, xyr, xyrs, xemself), documented from 1993; first used for an intersex person and now used for people of any gender.
Fae/Faer — A fairy-themed nounself set (fae, faer, faer, faers, faerself), created by 2013 and the most commonly used nounself pronoun set in 2021.
Ae/Aer — A set (ae, aer, aer, aers, aerself) created by David Lindsay for a third-sex alien race in the 1920 novel A Voyage to Arcturus.
Ey/Em — The Elverson pronouns (ey, em, eir, eirs, emself), created by Christine M. Elverson in 1975 as a gender-neutral set.
E/Em — The Spivak pronouns (E, em, eir, eirs, emself), used by Michael Spivak in The Joy of TeX so examples did not specify a person's gender.
One/One's — Standard English pronouns for a hypothetical person whose gender is not specified; a small number of people choose them for themselves.
It/Its — The standard genderless set for objects and animals; some nonbinary people reclaim it, but many consider it dehumanizing for a person unless they ask for it.
We/Us — Plural first-person pronouns, used when speaking for a group that includes the speaker.
Any — For people who have no one preferred set and are happy for others to use any pronouns for them.
