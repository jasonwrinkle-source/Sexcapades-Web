## Definitions index

**Profile.** One person's vector: for each of the 403 topics, a give value and a receive value, each from −100 to +100, with marks bolted on (hard limit, not interested, fantasy only, already tried). 806 components.

**Direction complements.** Give and receive are complements, never two flavors of one quantity. Nothing in this paper ever adds a person's give and receive together. Direction is never contracted into one number anywhere in the system, with one explicit exception stated in §4: the Personal Kink Factor takes the maximum flank per topic, signed, as its per-topic direction reading. That maximum is the sole permitted contraction over direction, by explicit switch, and it does not license any other.

**Tensor product (⊗).** The full grid of pairwise combinations of two profiles, every component of one laid against every component of the other. The product stage: everything, before anything decides what counts.

**Krossmatch (⋈).** The product restricted to matched components: each topic paired with itself, each direction paired with its complement. The operation of the sync between two people. [Session: wording to confirm — Krossmatch as outer product plus swap kernel, reading the crossed diagonal.]

**Complement map (κ).** Re-reads a profile in the crossed frame: (κQ) at topic t, direction d, equals Q at t with the direction swapped. The Krossmatch is the first profile laid component-wise against the complement of the second.

**Swap (σ).** The direction exchange: give exchanges with receive, and applying it twice returns home (σ² is the identity).

**Straight Match (⊙).** The product restricted to the parallel sector: each topic paired with itself, each direction paired with itself. The object of peer likeness. [Session: wording to confirm — straight as the same-direction diagonal, inner-product-like.]

**Straight Talk.** The shipped reading of the Straight Match between two people: per-topic straight sums, ranked, banded (§9).

**Contraction.** Summing an index away. A contracted index is a question answered in aggregate; a free index is an axis the reader still gets to see. The product stage builds the outer product; the contraction stage reads a diagonal and sums it — the inner-product-like step. Outer and inner are different stages, not different names for one thing. [Session: wording to confirm.]

**Overlay.** Co-display: both profiles shown together, nothing summed. Overlay answers no question; contraction answers one. The component table (§4) is the zero-contraction reading; an overlay is its visual cousin.

**Gates.** What may count, decided before any sum: hard limits (which remove components entirely), the route table (which decides what a component counts as), Heat (tonight's threshold), fantasy rules (Conversation only), and Veil (which gates what is seen, never what counts).

**Play Match Score (Play MS).** The Play outer number, the outer ring on a bowtie tab: the geometric-mean combination stated in §4, over Play-considered topics, fantasy excluded from numerator and denominator. [Flag: provisional definition, 2026-10-30, for consideration.] Formerly called the Match Score in this draft; the Play MS name governs.

**Personal Kink Factor (Personal KF).** The individual profile's Kink Factor, shown as the inner ring on the profile tab. It is a whole-map contraction, stated in §4: per topic, the maximum flank (signed, proportional, epsilon-thresholded) plus the flag sum once, hierarchically weighted and kinkiness-composed, additive in shape, over all 403 topics, not clamped at 100. [Flag: provisional definition, 2026-10-30, for consideration.]

**Charted.** The individual profile's outer ring on the profile tab. It is a coverage and attended share, stated in §4: per topic, once, binary rated (epsilon-thresholded, either flank) plus the flag sum once, hierarchically weighted and kinkiness-composed, over all 403 topics, no clamp. Charted is the name used for this ring throughout this revision. [Flag: provisional definition, 2026-10-30, for consideration.]

**Play Kink Factor (Play KF).** The Play inner number, the inner ring on a bowtie tab: the geometric-mean combination stated in §4, over shared non-limit-blocked topics. [Flag: provisional definition, 2026-10-30, for consideration.] The name Play KF supersedes Party KF, which is retired and is not used in this revision.

**Parlay Kink Factor (Parlay KF).** The straight inner number, the inner ring on a circledot tab, mirroring Play KF on the Straight Match. [Flag: provisional definition, 2026-10-30, for consideration. "Parlay" alone means nothing; the full name is always Parlay Kink Factor.]

**Parlay Match Score (Parlay MS).** The straight outer number, the outer ring on a circledot tab, mirroring Play MS on the Straight Match. The name Parlay Match Score is ruled, superseding the provisional working name Straight Match Score. Its definition is provisional, 2026-10-30, for consideration. [Flag: provisional definition.]

**Hot Topics.** The partial contraction: sum over people and channels, leave the topic index free — a ranked list per topic (§4).

**Lit sums (S, C).** S = gg + rr, the straight sum, whose contraction is resemblance; C = gr + rg, the crossed sum, whose contraction is fit (§10).

**Dark sums (K, ε).** K = gg − rr, the channel imbalance of a resemblance, unchanged when partners are exchanged; ε = gr − rg, the orientation of action-flow, which changes sign when partners are exchanged (§10).

**Heat as a phase.** A pairing-level angle, declared per session, never stored in a profile; only the difference of the two angles enters any score (§10, provisional in part: whether arousal is a third phase or a gain on Heat is open).

**Initiative.** Bookkeeping at the resolution layer, per live channel, not a contraction; a candidate, not built (§10, provisional).

**Hierarchical weights.** Each of the 18 categories carries an equal share; within a category, its direct topics and its subcategories split that share equally, direct topics being peers of subcategories; within a subcategory, its topics split the subcategory's share equally; a starburst splits among its topics. Weights apply to sums across topics, never to per-topic terms. The solver's energy is not weighted.

**Kinkiness.** One minus popularity, where popularity is prevalence, not acceptability. The effective weight of a topic in a calculation is its hierarchical share multiplied by its kinkiness, across all calculations, with the solver's energy excluded unless the owner says otherwise. Popularity itself is an aggregate over seven sources, weighted by statistical strength, stated in §4. [Flag: the strength numbers per source are not ruled, and the aggregated score's use as the Popularity List sort key is unverified in implementation; this paper states the rule, not a verified implementation.]

## 1. A profile is a vector

Take one person. For every topic in the taxonomy — 403 of them — they carry two numbers: how much they want to *give* it, and how much they want to *receive* it, each on a scale from −100 to +100. A profile is therefore a list of 806 numbers: a vector in an 806-dimensional space, one component per (topic, direction) pair. Bolted onto some components are marks — *hard limit*, *not interested*, *fantasy only*, *already tried* — flags that will matter later, when the vector is used.

Nothing about two people has been said yet. A single profile just sits there, the way a single vector does: complete, static, and entirely about one point of view.

One terminological commitment, made now because everything turns on it: the two directions are *complements*, not two flavors of one quantity. Nothing in what follows ever adds a person's give and receive together. The algebra keeps them separate at every stage, and the one operation that would blend them is the one operation the system refuses to perform. Direction is never contracted, in a profile, in a pair, or in a headline.

## 2. The product: every pairing at once

Comparing two people means forming their **tensor product**, written with the circled cross: Adam Peasant ⊗ Eve Vale. The tensor product of two vectors is not a single number and not a single new vector — it is the full grid of pairwise combinations, every component of one profile laid against every component of the other.

Most of that grid is meaningless (his rating for rope against her rating for latex says nothing). The sync lives on one thin, meaningful slice of it: for each topic, the two *live channels* — his give against her receive, and his receive against her give. That slice is a named object in its own right: **the Krossmatch**, written with the bowtie — Adam Peasant ⋈ Eve Vale — the product restricted to matched components, where a topic is paired with itself and a direction paired with its complement, never with itself. On each channel sits a raw component, the product of the two sides' values. The Krossmatch is the complete grid of what could flow between the two people, before anything decides what counts. (§8 examines the object itself.)

Components then resolve into outcomes. The strength of a pairing is set by the **weaker side**: his 100 against her 51 is a pairing of strength 51, because a scene happens at the level both people actually reach. Each live channel carries its own intensity, on the /100 scale of the ratings, per flank — the give-to-receive channel and the receive-to-give channel are never averaged into one number, because direction is never contracted. Each topic resolves, through a fixed route table over the two channels' signed values, into one of a small set of outcomes: Limit-blocked, Invisible, Match, Curious fit, Conflict, One-sided, Apart.

This table of per-topic outcomes is the Krossmatch's component list, resolved — the un-summed, fully detailed truth of the pair. Everything else the sync reports is a summary of this table, obtained by *contracting* it. Laid out without summing, the same table is an overlay: co-display, both profiles shown together, answering no question. Contraction is what turns the table into an answer.

## 3. The gates: why the contraction is not a pure sum

In Einstein's convention, contraction means summing over an index and letting it disappear. A naive contraction of the Krossmatch — just count the matches, divide by something, done — would be dishonest in specific, nameable ways, so the sum is **gated** before it is taken:

- **Hard limits remove Krossmatch components entirely.** A limited topic never enters any numerator. It is not averaged away, not outvoted — in a group it cannot be carried by majority, either. It stands in a visible, named list, outside the arithmetic. For a single person's own rings, a limit-only topic is excluded altogether from that person's calculation, in both rings, while the denominator stays at 403. The scored value therefore drops while the denominator does not shrink. That inconsistency is intentional and accepted: a hard limit carries a penalty in the headline, and the paper does not "fix" it by shrinking the denominator. A limit plus another mark is not covered by this rule; the limit-only rule is not extended automatically.
- **The route table decides what a component counts *as*.** A curious fit is not a small match; it is a different outcome class, and which classes enter which sum is a design decision, stated in advance.
- **Heat sets tonight's threshold.** Heat is a state, not a trait — a slider, bipolar around Neutral, set per person per session and never written into the profile. Raising it lets weaker and wider-gapped components count; lowering it below Neutral sheds curious fits first. Same tensor, different contraction, because the *occasion* changed.
- **Fantasy-only components contract only in Conversation mode.** In Play mode they are excluded; in Conversation they are fuel. Already Tried never changes a component's value at all — it annotates it, and in the Kink Factor tables it adds as a flag, as §4 states.
- **Veil is not a gate on the sum. It is a gate on the observer.** Each owner's Veil projects the Krossmatch onto what that owner is shown: their own veiled territory hides, the partner's shows deliberately as a teaser. Heat decides what counts; Veil decides what's seen.

## 5. What the language buys, and where it stops

The tensor language is not decoration. It enforces the design's central honesty: **every headline number can be un-contracted.** Un-contracting always lands in the same place: the Krossmatch, component by component — the grid of flows is where the receipts live. A score is a sum, and a sum can always be opened back into its components — the topics that voted, the gates they passed, the setting stamped on the result. A compatibility figure that cannot show its receipts is a horoscope; one that can is a measurement.

Where the analogy honestly stops: there is no geometry here. Topics are not coordinates on a smooth manifold; there is no distance between "rope" and "latex," no metric tensor in Einstein's sense, nothing that transforms covariantly. This is algebra, not differential geometry — a product, a set of gates, and a family of contractions. But that is enough. One object, honestly built, read in pairs and alone, with no reading allowed to hide how it was summed: that is the whole algorithm, and it fits in one line —

**profiles multiply; gates decide what may count; contractions decide what to ask.**

## 6. The open question: which contractions mean something

The product is not a design choice — two profiles multiply the same way regardless of what you intend to ask. All the design freedom lives in the contraction: which indices to sum away, and which to leave standing. A contracted index is a question answered in aggregate; a free index is an axis the reader still gets to see. Every statistic in §4 is one point in this space — leave the topic index free and you get Hot Topics; contract everything and you get a score. The general question the sync poses, for any statistic anyone proposes adding, is simply: which indices does it contract, and can its receipts still be shown?

One rule emerges from the cases that work: contract only the indices whose variation you are willing to stop seeing — and never contract an index before a reading that needs it. (Jason's amendment, 2026-10-21: the obvious candidate counterexample is the hypermap's own coloring — each category and subcategory colored by rolling its rated children up. But categories and subcategories carry direction too: the roll-up runs *within* each direction, give-children into the parent's give, receive into its receive, so the index actually summed is topic alone — children into parent — while direction stays free and positional at every level of the hierarchy. The heat map is therefore not an exception to the rule but an instance of it, one level up: a contraction over a coarser topic index, direction untouched.) Order matters, mathematically and not just presentationally. Sum a profile's give and receive components into a single number per topic, and you have destroyed, before any role question is asked, exactly the components in which roles live.

Roles are the clearest case. A role — Dominant, Submissive, Rigger, Brat — is a characteristic pattern over the same (topic, direction) components a profile carries: in the essay's language, a **prototype vector in the same 806-dimensional space**. Reading a person against the roles is then a contraction of one profile against each prototype, summing over topic and direction, with the **role index left free**. The result is a spectrum: one number per role, each normalized by the person's total rated mass, so it reads as the fraction of their rated self whose shape matches that prototype. (Familiar horoscope-style role tests print exactly this spectrum with the arithmetic hidden; here the arithmetic is the point.) And the partial version — topic index left free — returns the per-topic contributions, so every suggested role arrives with its receipts, as §5 demands of every number in the system.

One distinction of pairing deserves stating, because the formalism has exactly two. The sync's Krossmatch pairs direction *crossed* — give against receive — because it joins two people in complementary frames. The role reading pairs direction *straight* — the person's give against the prototype's give — because a prototype is not a partner across the table but a shape in the same frame. Crossed, for two people who might meet; straight, for one person held against a pattern.

A discovery, recorded the day the object was named (2026-10-21), extends the straight pairing to a second employment — and it arrived as a question about conversation. The Conversation readings of §4 are computed over the Krossmatch: they rank what two people could talk about *as prospective partners*. But conversation has two registers. **Crossed conversation** is negotiation talk — what could happen between us. **Straight conversation** is peer talk — two riggers comparing rope, two submissives comparing being tied — and it lives in exactly the components the Krossmatch discards: give against give, receive against receive. Play cannot use that parallel sector; nothing flows there. Talk can, because talk needs shared ground, not flow — and shared ground comes in both orientations. The formalism therefore has two pairing *kernels*, crossed and straight, and the straight kernel two employments: a person against a prototype (the roles above), and a person against a person in the peer register. The shipped Conversation mode now reads both registers: the crossed register in cross matches, and the straight register in straight matches, where it is shipped as Straight Talk (§9). The prototype employment remains what it always was here: a mirror, not a gate — a reading that contracts a single profile, speaks only to that person about themselves, is theirs to approve or discard, and never enters the sync between people.

Two honesties attach. First, the prototypes are themselves statistics — hand-authored at first, refinable later from profiles their owners donate for exactly that purpose — and a prototype built from a thin cohort carries real uncertainty, which an honest display encodes rather than hides. Profiles multiply; prototypes compare; and the choice of what to contract remains, as ever, the choice of what to ask.

## 7. The heat map: a contraction up the hierarchy

The pair product is not the only contraction in the system. A single profile contracts too, every time the hypermap colors a stem — and because categories and subcategories carry direction just as topics do, the contraction runs within each direction separately, exactly as §6's rule requires. No second profile takes part in it; there is no pairing here, and no Krossmatch — one profile, summed against its own hierarchy.

The index summed is topic, rolled upward through the hierarchy. A subcategory's give value is a per-direction aggregate of its topics' give ratings, counting only topics rated in that direction — unrated children are excluded, not zeroed. A category's value is the same aggregate taken over its *complete* topic list: it does not average its subcategories' averages, so every rated topic votes regardless of how the hierarchy groups it. [Session: confirm whether each aggregate is a mean or a sum — the exact formula is unverified in this pass and must be checked against the code before this paper states it.] No one rates a subcategory or category directly — that feature existed once and was deprecated, and the map is better without it. Where a stored value nevertheless sits at that level, it arrived as coarse import residue, and the shipped code does let such a value override the children's aggregate for display — a survival the owner has ruled against (see the design notes below): the per-direction aggregation is the tapers' universal characteristic, never to be superseded by a stored rating, and branch-level import seeds are to be resolved at topic level through the suggested-ratings popularity list rather than by standing in for their topics.

The aggregate is then read as a color — continuously, not in bands. Exactly zero is the curious yellow, pure. ## 8. The Krossmatch

§2 named the object in passing; this section examines it, because it is the joint the whole paper stands on.

The tensor product of two profiles is everything — every component of one against every component of the other. The Krossmatch is what you get by restricting that product along a fixed **pairing kernel**: the topic index is paired with itself, and the direction index is paired with its *complement*. Formally, let σ be the swap on directions — give exchanges with receive, and applying it twice returns you home: σ² is the identity. The kernel is then the identity on topics tensored with the swap on directions, and the Krossmatch's components are M_{t,d} = P_{t,d} · Q_{t,σ(d)}: for each topic, each direction, one person's value times the other's value in the opposite direction. Equivalently, define the **complement map** κ, which re-reads a profile in the crossed frame — (κQ)_{t,d} = Q_{t,σ(d)} — and the Krossmatch is the first profile laid component-wise against the complement of the second. The parallel components of the product — give against give, receive against receive — exist in the product and are simply not in the object. (They are not waste: §6 records the peer register of conversation that reads them.) The Krossmatch is defined by what it refuses as much as by what it keeps.

In the language of the definitions index: the Krossmatch is the outer product of the two profiles read through the swap kernel along the crossed diagonal. The outer product is the product stage; the diagonal read and its sum are the contraction stage. [Session: wording to confirm.]

The bowtie definition, as corrected by the owner on 2026-10-26, is stated here in his terms, with his caveat kept visible. The bowtie *is* a direct product with a kernel applied that swaps give and receive on one profile, and the crossed contraction follows: my give times your receive, plus my receive times your give. The straight reading is the same direct product without the swap, with same-direction terms. The owner states this understanding with the caveat, in his own words, that he may be using a wrong language there. This paper therefore records the definition as stated and flags it for the session to test, rather than asserting it as settled mathematics if the mathematics does not support the wording. An earlier instruction to sweep direct-product language from the paper is withdrawn and is not applied here.

In arithmetic, with no machinery at all: a topic where Adam Peasant stands at give 80, receive 10 and Eve Vale at give 20, receive 90 crosses to 80×90 + 10×20 = 7,400. Make Eve Vale a top instead — give 90, receive 20, the same total appetite for the same topic — and the crossing reads 80×20 + 10×90 = 2,500. No rule anywhere says two tops fit worse. The multiplication is the rule: when both people lean the same way, every product in the sum contains a small factor, and the fit starves on its own.

Summed whole, the Krossmatch induces a bilinear form on profiles, B(P, Q) = Σ_{t,d} P_{t,d} · Q_{t,σ(d)}, and the form has a signature worth naming. Rotate the direction plane by 45 degrees, into the **blend** axis — (give + receive)/√2 — and the **difference** axis — (give − receive)/√2 — and the form reads B = blend·blend − difference·difference. In direction space, per topic, its signature is (1, −1): indefinite, the way spacetime's interval is indefinite, though nothing here is a spacetime. The decomposition says in one line what the arithmetic example says in numbers: agreement in overall appetite adds to a pair's fit; agreement in directional lean subtracts from it. Opposites fit — not as a slogan, but as the signature of the only pairing the sync performs.

The summed form is symmetric: exchanging the partners leaves B unchanged, because the sum runs over every topic and both directions. The flanks are not symmetric — exchanging the partners swaps the give-to-receive channel with the receive-to-give channel — and §10's dark sums are exactly the instruments that hear that asymmetry. Symmetry of the sum, asymmetry of the channels: both facts, priced separately.

Physicists will recognize the shape of the choice. Indices can be contracted through an identity, which pairs each thing with itself, or through a pairing tensor, which pairs each thing with its dual — spinor indices are contracted through the antisymmetric ε, not through a metric, and that choice is what makes a spinor's partner its complement rather than its copy. The Krossmatch is the same species of decision, made once and made everywhere: directions are contracted through the swap, so that each direction's dual is the other. Give has no meaning in this algebra except against receive, and the pairing is where that is enforced.

Two separations keep the object honest. First, the Krossmatch is **not gated**: it is pure bilinear algebra, the raw grid of flows. The weaker-side rule, the route table, the limits, Heat — §3's whole apparatus — are nonlinear semantics applied to its components afterward, and conflating the two layers would make the algebra impossible to audit. Second, the Krossmatch is **one of exactly two pairing kernels** in the formalism. The other kernel pairs direction straight, and it carries two employments: the role reading of §6, where a prototype is a shape in the same frame rather than a partner in the opposite one, and the peer register of conversation, where the product's parallel sector is read as shared ground between two people. Everything else in the system that looks like a pairing — the heat map of §7 — is no pairing at all, but a single profile summed against itself.

A scope note, for the same reason: as constructed, the Krossmatch is pairwise. Group readings (§4) gather pair Krossmatches under majority rule, and the construction plainly suggests the generalization — each profile crossed against the rest — but a single group-native object, one tensor for *n* profiles, is not claimed here as well-defined. The pair object is exact; the group form works theoretically, and the paper keeps the distinction visible.

That is the object's power and its limit. It says nothing about what a pair should do; it is the complete, un-gated statement of what could flow between them, computed by the one arithmetic that refuses, structurally, to pretend that wanting the same side is the same as fitting. Every verdict the sync delivers is a gated contraction of this grid — and when a verdict is questioned, this is the grid it answers to.

## 9. The Straight Match

§8 closed by separating the Krossmatch from the only other pairing kernel in the formalism. This section gives that kernel its object, because it has one — as definite as the Krossmatch, and disjoint from it.

Take the product of two profiles and look at one topic at a time. Where a topic meets itself, the product's components form a 2×2 block over the direction pairs: give against give, give against receive, receive against give, receive against receive. The block has exactly two sectors. The **diagonal** — give against give, receive against receive — pairs each direction with itself: the *parallel* sector. The **anti-diagonal** — give against receive, receive against give — pairs each direction with its complement: the *crossed* sector. The two sectors share no component, and between them they exhaust the block: per topic, the product's self-pairing is exactly these two sectors and nothing else.

The Krossmatch is the anti-diagonal, kept by the pairing kernel of §8: identity on topics, swap on directions. The diagonal is kept by the mirror-image choice — identity on topics *and* identity on directions, pairing every index with itself through the Kronecker delta, where the Krossmatch pairs direction through the swap. The object it defines is the **Straight Match**, written with the circled dot: Adam Peasant ⊙ Eve Vale. Its components are S_{t,d} = P_{t,d} · Q_{t,d} — for each topic, each direction, one person's value times the other's value *in the same direction*. Algebraists know the operation as the Hadamard product: the component-wise product of two arrays read in the same frame. Where the Krossmatch lays a profile against the complement of another, the Straight Match lays it against the other exactly as it stands.

The two objects are one operation viewed twice, and the notation can say so: **P ⋈ Q = P ⊙ κQ** — the Krossmatch is the Straight Match computed with the second profile crossed first, by the complement map of §8. There is a single primitive here — multiply matching components — and a single frame choice, straight or crossed, that decides what *matching* means. A pairing is a choice of what counts as a match; everything else in this section is bookkeeping for that choice. In the language of the definitions index: the straight reading is the same-direction diagonal, inner-product-like; the crossed reading is the outer product through the swap kernel. [Session: wording to confirm.]

Sum each object whole and the bookkeeping pays out in two different currencies. The Straight Match contracts to the ordinary inner product ⟨P, Q⟩ — a positive-definite form, the Euclidean geometry of the profile space itself. It is a *similarity* measure, and agreement feeds it without exception: two components pointing the same way multiply to a positive term whether what is shared is appetite or aversion, because in a single frame likeness is likeness — in lean as much as in appetite. The Krossmatch contracts to B(P, Q) of §8, indefinite, signature (1, −1): agreement in appetite adds, and agreement in lean subtracts. Similarity and fit are different facts, and the formalism now prices them separately. Two tops carrying *identical* maps are the limiting case: maximally similar — the Straight Match reads as high as the scale allows — and minimally fit, their crossing starving on small factors exactly as §8's arithmetic showed. A single compatibility number would have to call that pair both perfect and hopeless. The two pairings decline to confuse the two statements; there is a number for each fact.

The Straight Match is no longer idle. **Straight Talk** is its shipped reading between two people, in Conversation mode only and in straight matches only, never in Play and never in a crossed match: for each shared topic, the straight sum — give×give plus receive×receive, limits excluded, a null sum where neither direction is known on both sides — ranked highest first, ties by topic name, and banded over equal thirds of the per-topic range (−20,000 to +20,000): Debate at −6,667 and below, On-the-fence from −6,666 to +6,666, Shop talk at +6,667 and above, no band for a null sum. The rings wear the band names with their counts, and a Limits section stands beside them, display only. What Straight Talk reads is what two people hold in common, in the same orientation — the conversation register §6 discovered in the sector the sync had always discarded, now computed in the open. Peer-ground is dropped as a term; it is not used in this revision. The prototype employment stays where §6 put it: a mirror reading against a pattern, not a gate, and not a shipped score.

Straight Talk's headline follows §4. Debate topics count in the Parlay MS numerator and denominator, like Shop talk and On-the-fence topics. Bands display sections only; none of the four pair numbers refers to banded topics. [Flag: provisional, as in §4.]

## 10. The Penumbra

§§8 and 9 leave no third sector hiding in the product. For each topic, the product's 2×2 direction block splits into the diagonal and anti-diagonal sectors — parallel and crossed, disjoint and together exhaustive. The penumbra begins one level up, when those components are summed. A sum can read a sector with a plus sign, or read it against itself; both choices are arithmetically real, and only one of them is lit.

Suppress the topic index and write the four products as *gg*, *rr*, *gr*, and *rg*: give against give, receive against receive, give against receive, receive against give. There are four independent signed sums. Two are already the paper's working quantities: **S = gg + rr**, the straight sum, whose contraction is resemblance; and **C = gr + rg**, the crossed sum, whose contraction is fit. The engine reads this lit pair. The other two are **K = gg − rr**, the channel imbalance of a resemblance, and **ε = gr − rg**, the orientation of action-flow. K is unchanged when the partners are exchanged. ε is not: exchanging them exchanges *gr* and *rg*, so ε changes sign. It records which way the current runs, a fact no symmetric sum can contain.

Darkness, then, is not a property of hidden components. The four products sit in the product in plain sight. It is a choice of signs at the moment of summation — which combinations an instrument reads, and which it leaves standing unread in the penumbra.

The receipt is arithmetic. Let one person stand at give 80, receive 20, and the other at give 30, receive 90. The crossed products are 80 × 90 = 7,200 and 30 × 20 = 600, so C = 7,800 and ε = 6,600. Reverse the directional lean — give 30, receive 90 against give 80, receive 20 — and the same two products change places: C is still 7,800, while ε is −6,600. Addition cannot tell the pairs apart; the dark sum can. Same crossed fit, opposite action-flow.

K answers a different question. Two people alike chiefly as givers, each at give 90 and receive 10, have S = 8,200 and K = 8,000. Two people alike chiefly as receivers, each at give 10 and receive 90, have the same S and K = −8,000. The straight sum rightly calls the resemblances equal. K says that they live in opposite channels — a distinction peer talk would hear immediately, even though no shipped score reports it.

**Heat as a phase.** The lit sums are timeless: they describe two profiles, not the occasion on which the profiles meet. Heat was salvaged as a pairing-level weather by scoping it carefully. It does not complexify a profile. Saved profiles remain lists of real magnitudes, exactly as §1 defines them. At a pairing, each person wears one *declared* angle for the session — a garment over the profile, not a change to it — and the pairing is read in the relative angle. Under the sesquilinear reading, the lit scores pick up the alignment factor cos(Δθ), where Δθ is the difference of the two angles. A single angle is unobservable alone; only the difference enters any score, which is why Heat belongs to the pairing and cannot be stored as one person's trait.

The clock is global: one estrus clock per person, with two scales. The slow hand is the estrous cycle, turning over weeks. It may be informed by a tracker, and it modulates the fast hand — Heat proper, the declared angle of tonight, turning over days. Where a tracker and a declaration disagree, the declaration takes precedence: the person is the authority on their own state, and a tracker is an informed prior, never an imposed reading. At the Clarity seam, the fast hand does not glide through zero; desire resets by a jump, sometimes into aversion. When one partner has crossed that seam and the other has not, Δθ approaches π, cos(Δθ) approaches −1, and the pairing *inverts* rather than merely cancelling. The shipped Heat slider is the fast hand's ancestor: a session-only, declared threshold state, never written into a saved profile, reporting the same weather in a coarser instrument.

One hand remains undecided, and the section leaves it open on purpose. Arousal — the within-the-evening variable — might be a third phase: then the session garment is a 3-torus, arousal carries an angle of its own, and it could invert an alignment by itself. Or arousal might be a gain on the heat hand: then the garment is a 2-torus with an amplitude, arousal can raise or lower the size of a reading but never reverse its sign. The mathematics permits both; which one the body is doing is an open question, and the formalism will not pretend otherwise.

**Initiative.** Not every named quantity in the system is a contraction, and Initiative needs no second algebra. It is bookkeeping at the resolution layer — computed only after §3's gates have decided which channels are live, and attributed per channel rather than summed away. For a live channel from a giver to a receiver, define the **surplus** as the giver's give minus the receiver's receive. Rectify it: a positive surplus attributes initiative to the giver, whose wanting overflows the appetite it meets; a negative surplus attributes initiative of the same magnitude to the receiver, whose appetite summons the scene. The totals keep their territory partition — a surplus earned on one topic is not cancelled by an opposite surplus on another, the way ε's terms cancel. Initiative says *where* each person's wanting is doing the driving, topic by topic.

The worked check is the one that forced the distinction. In a teacher/student scene, on the discipline channel, let the teacher's give be 70 and the student's receive be 95. The surplus is 70 − 95 = −25: initiative to the student, 25. The teacher may hold the authority of the role entire; the scene's unfolding can still be driven by the student's appetite. Authority and initiative are different facts, and this bookkeeping is the first instrument in the design that can say so in numbers.

Initiative is therefore named and formalized here as a *candidate*, not built in the app. Two questions remain the owner's to settle before it can be more: whether surplus is the right attribution rule at all, against the other rules the record sketches; and whether initiative earned on Curious-fit channels — the explorative frontier, where the wanting is newest — should carry extra weight.
