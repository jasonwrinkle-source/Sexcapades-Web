## 1. Abstract & Scope

**Purecapades** maps human positions on pure and theoretical mathematics — what math is, what makes it true, how it is proved, what makes it beautiful, which branch is home, and how you live as a mathematician. In traditional surveys, mathematical attitudes are collapsed into crude single-axis binaries (e.g. Good at math vs. Bad at math) or reduced to a list of courses taken.

Under the **Taxopolemics** engine, mathematical positions are mapped to their natural geometric degrees of freedom: from **1-Pole scalars** (absolute affinity), to **2-Pole dialectical dipoles** (Constructive vs. Classical; Rigor vs. Intuition), to higher-order **Triads (3-Pole $\Delta^2$)**, **Tetrads (4-Pole $\Delta^3$)**, **Pentads (5-Pole $\Delta^4$)**, **Hexads (6-Pole $\Delta^5$)**, **Heptads (7-Pole $\Delta^6$)**, and **Octads (8-Pole $\Delta^7$)**.

**Engine cap:** the engine goes to **8 poles and caps there** — monopole through octad. The cap is a human limit, not a technical one: people cannot hold more than about eight positions in mind at once (Jason, 2026-11-14).

**Scope note:** Abstracapades is the ninth skin, and the first of a planned pair. It covers pure and theoretical mathematics. Applied mathematics is a separate skin (name to be decided — Applied-Mathcapades or Modelcapades) — the two are different cultures with different values, and a single skin would dilute both. The pure/applied divide is itself a topic in this skin (4.3). Abstracapades brushes Philoscapades (philosophy of mathematics — Platonism, formalism, and the nature of mathematical truth live in epistemology and metaphysics); this skin goes deeper into the specifically mathematical positions, the way Dharmacapades goes deeper than Forescapades. The audience is narrower than the other skins — mathematicians, physicists, and math-adjacent people — but the positions are real and passionate, and for collaborators and co-authors they determine whether you can work together.

**Name note:** The skin was first drafted as "Purecapades" (2026-11-15). The Principal Investigator hated that name. He first accepted "Pure-Mathcapades" / "Applied-Mathcapades" as clear, then changed his mind within the minute: "I like Axiomacapades... abstracapades even more." The name is **Abstracapades** — abstraction is the essence of pure math, and the coinage fits the family's style. Axiomacapades was the runner-up. The applied skin's name is still to be decided.

## 2. Taxonomic Architecture & Rating Widget Assignments

### Category 1: Foundations

#### 1.1 Foundations of Mathematics (5-Pole Pentad $\Delta^4$)
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Blue #3b82f6)`: **Platonism** — Mathematical objects exist independently; we discover them (Plato, Gödel).
  - `Pole 1 (Gray #6b7280)`: **Formalism** — Math is symbols and rules; meaning is irrelevant (Hilbert).
  - `Pole 2 (Green #22c55e)`: **Intuitionism** — Math is a construction of the mind; only what can be built is real (Brouwer).
  - `Pole 3 (Purple #a855f7)`: **Logicism** — Math reduces to logic; all truths are logical truths (Frege, Russell).
  - `Pole 4 (Orange #f97316)`: **Structuralism** — Math is about structures and patterns, not objects (Benacerraf, Shapiro).
- **Question:** What is mathematics, at bottom?
- **Optimal Widget:** Pentad radial star.

#### 1.2 Discovered vs Invented (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Discovered (math is out there, we find it) ↔ Invented (math is made up by humans)
- **Question:** Is math discovered or invented?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 1.3 Infinity: Actual vs Potential (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Actual infinity (a completed thing, Cantor) ↔ Potential infinity (a process, never finished, Aristotle)
- **Question:** Is infinity a thing, or just a direction?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 2: Proof & Rigor

#### 2.1 Constructive vs Classical (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Constructive (no law of excluded middle; you must build it) ↔ Classical (excluded middle ok; proof by contradiction ok)
- **Question:** Do you accept the law of excluded middle?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 2.2 Computer Proof (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Accept (a computer-assisted proof is a real proof) ↔ Reject (if a human can't check it, it isn't a proof)
- **Question:** Is the Four Color Theorem proved?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 2.3 Rigor vs Intuition (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Rigor (formal proof, no gaps) ↔ Intuition (insight, the feel of it)
- **Question:** Do you trust formal rigor, or mathematical intuition?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 3: Beauty & Elegance

#### 3.1 Elegance vs Power (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Elegance (a beautiful proof) ↔ Power (a powerful result)
- **Question:** What makes good math — a beautiful proof, or a powerful theorem?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 3.2 Simplicity vs Depth (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Simplicity (clean, spare results) ↔ Depth (rich, complex structures)
- **Question:** Do you value simple results or deep ones?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 3.3 Beauty: Essential vs Incidental (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Essential (beauty is a sign of truth) ↔ Incidental (beauty is a nice bonus)
- **Question:** Is beauty in math a guide to truth, or just a pleasure?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 4: Branches & Territory

#### 4.1 Algebra vs Geometry vs Analysis (3-Pole Triad $\Delta^2$)
- **Poles:**
  - `Pole 0 (Apex)`: **Algebra** — Structure, symbols, equations.
  - `Pole 1 (Base Right)`: **Geometry** — Shape, space, form.
  - `Pole 2 (Base Left)`: **Analysis** — Limits, continuity, the infinite small.
- **Question:** Which branch is home?
- **Optimal Widget:** Barycentric Equilateral Triangle Widget.

#### 4.2 Discrete vs Continuous (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Discrete (combinatorics, number theory, graphs) ↔ Continuous (analysis, topology, geometry)
- **Question:** Do you live in the discrete world or the continuous one?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 4.3 Pure vs Applied (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Pure (math for its own sake) ↔ Applied (math for what it does)
- **Question:** The skin-defining divide — where do you stand?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 5: Abstraction & Intuition

#### 5.1 Abstraction: High vs Low (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** High abstraction (category theory, the general) ↔ Low abstraction (concrete computation, the particular)
- **Question:** How abstract should math be?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 5.2 Visual vs Symbolic (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Visual (pictures, diagrams, geometry) ↔ Symbolic (formulas, algebra, symbols)
- **Question:** Do you think in pictures or in symbols?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 5.3 Intuition: Trust vs Distrust (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Trust (intuition guides you right) ↔ Distrust (intuition misleads; only proof counts)
- **Question:** Do you trust mathematical intuition?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

### Category 6: The Mathematical Life

#### 6.1 Collaboration vs Solitude (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Collaboration (math with others) ↔ Solitude (math alone)
- **Question:** Do you do math alone, or with others?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 6.2 Publication vs Perfection (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Publish quickly ↔ Polish forever
- **Question:** Do you publish fast, or perfect first?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

#### 6.3 Teaching vs Research (2-Pole Dipole)
- **Poles:** Teaching (passing it on) ↔ Research (pushing it forward)
- **Question:** Do you value teaching or research more?
- **Optimal Widget:** Dipole slider.

## 4. Higher-Pole Candidates (not yet specified)

- **The Branches** (hexad): Algebra / Geometry / Analysis / Topology / Number Theory / Logic. An expansion of 4.1 from a triad to a hexad, covering all six major branches. Needs a ruling on whether the triad or the hexad is the right granularity.
- **Proof Methods** (pentad): Direct / Contradiction / Induction / Construction / Diagonalization. The ways you prove things, as a multi-pole topic. Needs a ruling on whether methods are positions or just techniques.
- **Famous Problems** (octad): Riemann Hypothesis / P vs NP / Goldbach / Twin Primes / Navier-Stokes / Hodge / Birch-Swinnerton-Dyer / Yang-Mills. The Millennium Problems plus the classics, as an 8-pole topic (which ones do you care about?). Needs a ruling on whether problems are positions or just interests — and whether an octad of problems is meaningful or just a list.
