# Briefing — The Types of TaxoPolemic Hypermaps

Notes drafted 2026-11-22 (Jason & YoBiche). Status per type is marked
honestly: two types are built and live, three are envisioned only.

## What makes a type a type

The engine is one: topics arranged as a hypermap, each topic rated on
poles, an independent signed intensity carried alongside as the
(n+1)th number, profiles as vectors, and three operations over them —
Cross ⋈, Straight ⊙, Circle Minus ⊖. None of that changes between
types.

A **type** is fixed by one thing: *what the number means.*

- On a stance map the number is a position. It cannot be false, only opposed.
- On an action map the number is an appetite. It cannot be false, only unmatched.
- On a mastery map the number would be a capability. It is the first
  kind of number in the family that can be *false*.
- On a need map the number is a requirement or a capacity. It can be unmet.
- On a drift map the number is a movement. It is measured, not held.

Everything else — widgets, layout, the tensor, Scribe — is shared
machinery that each type inherits. A new subject (a new skin) is cheap.
A new type is a new claim about what a rating *is*, and should be
rarer.

## Type 1 — Stance maps (BUILT)

**Topics** are propositions: positions a person or a document can hold.
**Placements** are Advocacy and Receptivity — the mouth and the ears.
Advocacy is putting the stance into the commons; Receptivity is taking
a stance in — hearing it, learning from it, letting it discipline your
own claims. Receptivity is not itself a stance. Each placement carries
its own intensity, and each can go negative on its own account:
negative Advocacy is shrinking from pressing the issue; negative
Receptivity is refusing to take it in, which Circle Minus reads as a
stronger feud signal than an absent ring.

The transmission relation lives here, natively: advocates are the
teachers and receptors are the students, in the only sense a stance
map needs. Cross reads one profile's Advocacy against the other's
Receptivity — the teaching channel, whether this voice can reach those
ears. Straight reads mouth to mouth and ears to ears. Minus counts the
splits, refused hearings included.

**Skins:** Poliscapades, Forescapades, Philoscapades, Aesthcapades
(taste is a stance), Vocapades (inclination), Techcapades,
Dharmacapades, Theoricapades, Praxicapades, Quantacapades,
Expericapades, Simulcapades, Mattercapades, Chemcapades,
Nouscapades. Meter words vary per skin (stance, belief, position,
conviction, understanding, …); on vanilla skins the Kink Factor's
equivalent is the Depth Factor.

## Type 2 — Action maps (BUILT)

**Topics** are practices: things people do together, where direction
matters. **Flanks** are give and receive, never averaged or mixed —
direction is never contracted, in profiles, roll-ups, tooltips, or
statistics. The rating is an appetite, and the matching problem is the
live one: who wants to do this, in which direction, how much.

Cross is the crossed fit (my give against your receive), Straight is
the shared appetite, Minus is the clash of direction or desire.

**Skins:** Sexcapades (the original), Dancecapades (lead/follow),
Questcapades (run/play). These three are the maps whose topics are
verbs between people.

## Type 3 — Mastery maps (ENVISIONED — type status unresolved)

The teaching *relation* is already covered by stance maps (above), so
a mastery map is not needed to represent teachers and students. What
it would add is the one axis stance cannot hold: **capability itself**
— can-do and can-teach as claims about performance, independent of
what the person argues or will hear. A fervent advocate with open ears
may still be unable to do the derivation; the stance map has nowhere
to write that down.

Shape, if built: a mastery skin would be a *twin* of a stance skin —
same roots, new canopy. Categories and branch structure identical
(which keeps cross-type operations computable at branch level), but
the leaves re-authored as performances: named results carry over
almost as-is (Bell's theorem: derive the inequality, explain the
experiment), stance triads become can-you-state-each-account topics,
pure-opinion leaves drop out, and technique leaves stance never
needed appear (propagate the error bars, align the apparatus). The
four physics stance maps (Quanta, Matter, Theori, Experi) are the
natural first family of twins. Operations turn practical: Cross
between student and professor is supervision fit; Minus between a
student and the whole field is a syllabus — the uncovered ground, in
order.

Open question, recorded rather than resolved: whether capability
deserves a type of its own, or is a thinner layer over subjects the
family already maps. If built at all, its topics are performances —
structurally nearer the action maps than the stance twins it would
shadow.

## Type 4 — Need maps (ENVISIONED)

**Topics** are requirements and capacities. One flank is what I need,
the other is what I can offer; intensity stops being enthusiasm and
becomes urgency. The matching problem is allocation, not
compatibility: Cross is supply meeting demand, Straight is shared
need or shared surplus, Minus is *unmet need* — the ground one side
requires that nobody in the group covers.

Candidate homes: a household, a care network, a mutual-aid group, a
response crew. It is the action map's sober cousin — same directional
skeleton, but the numbers are obligations rather than desires, and an
unrated topic reads as "not assessed," never as "not needed."

## Type 5 — Drift maps (ENVISIONED — a meta type)

A drift map has no taxonomy of its own. It consumes **timestamped
profiles from any other skin** and renders trajectories instead of
positions: each rating a track, the old position ghosted, the current
one solid. Its placements are tenses — what was held, what is held,
optionally what the holder thinks should be held.

The mathematics already exists: a Circle Minus between two profiles
of the *same entity at two dates* is a drift readout. The engine does
not care that both profiles are the same party; it reports where they
split and how hard. The build cost of a drift skin is therefore not
the math but the plumbing and the rendering — snapshot dates, tracks,
ghosts — plus the one reading no current map has: **convergence**.
Run two entities' drift tracks against each other and the headline
number is whether their maps are moving toward each other or apart,
regardless of where either stands today.

### Prototype, 2026-11-22

As a first taste, the 2016 Democratic Party platform was rated on the
Poliscapades taxonomy (same topics and conventions as the app's
current Democratic platform sample; receptivity derived at the same
0.40 factor the existing samples use) and set against the current
sample:

- 37 topics rated in both, mean movement **6.4 points** per pole.
- Largest mover: **Deportation** (37 points) — in 2016, prioritized
  removal was still an accepted instrument (+30); the current sample
  rates it −65, with the position consolidated on removal after fair
  process.
- **Trade Policy** moved 13: protective tariffs 30 → 50, free trade
  25 → 10 — the fair-trade centre held while the poles swung.
- **Death penalty**: abolition 85 → 70. The 2016 platform's plank was
  maximal ("It has no place in the United States of America"); the
  current platform dropped the plank, and the sample's rating softened
  with it.
- **Fossil fuels**: phase-out 40 → 60, and the topic's intensity
  flipped sign (−60 → +60) — in 2016 the strongest holding on the
  topic was opposition to drilling; now it is the phase-out itself.
- **School Choice**: charter schools −35 → −60 — the party cooled on
  charters while holding public investment steady.
- **The Middle East** narrowed toward parity: stand with Israel
  55 → 40, Palestinian self-determination 35 → 45.
- Three topics in the current sample have **no 2016 plank at all** —
  Birthright citizenship, Electoral Systems, Digital Currency. They
  arrived on the agenda between the two platforms.

Ratings and readout: `dem2016-ratings.json` and the drift table were
produced as working papers for this note; the 2016 ratings are a
first-pass reading of the platform text and are open to correction
topic by topic.
