The app now keeps itself current: every load checks for the newest version, and installed copies update themselves. You should never be stuck on an old version — and you never need to clear your browser’s cache or delete browsing data for this site.

One exception: if you installed the app or visited before October 20, 2026, your device may still hold one older copy. A hard refresh — a reload that forces the browser to fetch the fresh copy instead of reusing the stored one — clears it, once. After that, the app stays current on its own:

  • Windows or Linux: press Ctrl + Shift + R.
  • Mac: press Cmd + Shift + R.
  • Chromebook: press Ctrl + Shift + R.
  • Phone or tablet: close the app (or the browser tab) completely, then reopen it.
  • If the page still won’t load at all, check your connection and try again in a few minutes.

A current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari is enough. See the guide.

Sexcapades uses fourtwo separate color systems. Their meanings overlap in places, but they are not interchangeable:

  1. Rating slider colors describe how you rated a topic. These same ratings appear on the map’s At-a-Glance rings and in list view:
    GreenWant to do / interested
    YellowCurious / maybe
    RedNot interested / limit
  2. Veil colors show the active interest-threshold filter, not a new rating. Red is selective and keeps only your strongest interests; yellow is the middle; green is open and shows the most topics. Changing the veil only filters what is visible.
  3. Hanky code colors identify activities, not your level of interest. The hanky code is a traditional handkerchief color-coding system that originated in gay and biker culture for signaling sexual interests. A person might wear a colored handkerchief in a back pocket at a bar, club, munch, or play party so others can recognize those interests. Sexcapades uses those traditional colors on topic branches where a matching code exists. Open the hanky code chart.
  4. Selection cyan is not a rating or a score: a cyan outline marks the profile tabs you have selected, and the app mints a group profile from the selection. There is no strictness control — to narrow or widen what a comparison shows, use the Veil (above).

Archetypes and Samples are profiles you can try without rating everything yourself. Archetypes are standard positions the map was built from — a Sadist or a Rope Bunny on Sexcapades, a party platform on Poliscapades, a denominational creed on Forescapades.a party platform, a denominational creed, or a school of thought, depending on the map. Samples are real people who shared their map — Adam Peasant, Eve Vale, and BothInches on Sexcapades. Pick one from the menu and the map fills with its ratings; compare it with yours like any profile.

Some archetypes come as a stack of dated layers — a party platform across election years, a creed across centuries. A stack is a single menu entry; picking it opens a flyout of its layers, oldest first and the current one last, ending with Load whole stack. Load one layer to see the position at that date, or load the whole stack to read how it moved over time — the stack is a palimpsest, and the guide’s Select profiles walkthrough covers the readings: drift, formation, and persistence.

An archetype’s picture in the center of the map is its emblem — the tradition’s or party’s own symbol, like the Methodist cross and flame or the Democratic donkey — not a photo. Every layer of a stack wears the same emblem. Samples of real people keep their own photos.

A roster pack is one file holding several ready-made profiles for a single map — a whole group, packed together. Import it through Scan or Import and every member arrives as its own profile, ready to compare. Each roster also appears as its own entry in the app’s + menu, opening to its members. A pack belongs to its skin: it loads only on that map, and the app will not open it on another. Questcapades ships with a demo roster, the Friday Knights.

Still stuck?

These three checks solve most problems.

  1. Hard-refresh the page (Ctrl + Shift + R) so you’re viewing the current version.
  2. Confirm your import came directly from a Sexcapades export or sync card.
  3. Keep an original Sexcapades PNG, SVG, PDF, or JSON export as your permanent backup.
