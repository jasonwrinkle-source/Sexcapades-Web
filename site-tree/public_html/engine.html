TaxoPolemics

One engine. Eighteen hypermaps. The hard conversations, mapped.

Some questions have two sides. Some have eight. The talks that matter most are the ones we keep dodging — politics at the table, faith, the big questions. This is a way to have them.

capade — noun. An adventure; an exhibition. (From Ice Capades, itself a pun on escapade: a daring undertaking that goes against convention.) Each map here is a capade in both senses — an adventure in a contested subject, and an exhibition of where people stand on it. TaxoPolemics is the engine that builds them.

How to enter

Pick a map. Each map opens on its own gate page — the front door for that conversation. From the gate, open the hypermap to walk the map, or the Hot List to see what is hot. That is the whole flow: picker, gate, hypermap or Hot List.

Only one map is adults-only. Sexcapades asks you to confirm you are 18 or older before it lets you in. The other fifteen open straight through.

The idea: an indaba, drawn as a map

A hard talk is a dialectic — positions in tension, each with something true in it. The engine treats it as an indaba: in isiZulu and isiXhosa, a matter of importance, and the gathering called to talk it through — until everyone has been heard and a common mind emerges.

The usual way to have a hard talk is to argue until somebody wins. This is the other way: draw the positions out, stand in yours, and look at the whole picture together.

What you get is a hypermap — the whole indaba on one picture, every position drawn out, so you can stand in yours and see the rest.

Nothing here is a test. There is no scorecard and no winner. The map shows where people stand — not who is right.

The poles

Every hard question has poles: the places to stand on it. A yes-or-no has two. Some questions have three, or five, or eight. The engine counts the poles and asks them all — every side of the question, not just the two loudest.

You say where you stand on each pole. The map draws it: branches on a wheel, every spoke a question, every color how you feel about it.

What it measures

Depth Factor
How deep you stand in what you believe. The roots underneath, not the volume on top.
Charted
How much of the map you have walked. The ground your feet have been.
Crossmatch
Lay two maps together, every topic crossed, and see where they overlap. Where our maps overlap, that is common ground. The Match Score counts it.

None of it is a grade. It is a mirror, held up to what you know — and a way to find the ground you share before you argue about the ground you don't.

Who it is for

Two people at a table. Friends who disagree. A group that wants to know itself. Anyone with a talk they have been putting off.

Pull up a chair, put the kettle on. No test, no scorecard — just "what do you think?" and "tell me more."
